New to ILCA

Moving up from Optimist or Topper? This is the guide we wish we'd had: what to buy, what it really costs, how to get the boat to events and where to find them.

  • For sailors and parents
  • Read 10 min
  • Updated Nov 2026

    The short version

    • Sail number tells you a boat's age better than the advert does.
    • A tidy, competitive hull matters more than new sails in your first season.
    • Budget for entries, travel and a second sail, not just the boat.
    • Get on the water at club open training before your first open meeting.

    Before you buy

    Every ILCA uses the same hull, so the choice is really about which rig and how old a boat. Most sailors coming out of Optimists start on the ILCA 4 rig; heavier or older Topper sailors sometimes go straight to the ILCA 6.

    The hull number on the transom (and stamped under the deck) dates the boat. Higher numbers are newer. Hulls get softer and heavier with age, which matters most in a breeze and in chop.

    Quick check: sail number vs age

    A rough guide we use when looking at adverts. Verify ranges with ILCA UK / builder records.

    Hull numberRough ageGood for
    220000+0–5 yearsRacing at national level and up

    Questions to ask the seller

    • Has the hull ever been repaired? Where?
    • Which rig parts come with it (lower masts, booms, sails)?
    • Is there a trolley, road trailer and covers?
    • When were the foils last faired?

    What a good price looks like

    Prices move with the season: you'll usually do better buying in autumn than in April. Use these as a starting point, not a quote.

    £2–3.5kOlder club boat
    £4–6kCompetitive second-hand
    £8k+New, full kit

    Price ranges are placeholders. Check against current adverts before publishing.

    Sails, entries and running costs

    The boat is the big cheque. The season is the steady drip. A typical first year for a sailor doing club racing and a handful of opens includes:

    ItemRough costHow often
    Sail£400–600One a season if racing hard
    Open meeting entry£40–80Per event
    Championship entry£150–300Per event
    Class membership£30–60Yearly

    All costs to verify.

    Towing, roof-racking and strapping

    An ILCA hull fits on most car roofs, and plenty of families never own a trailer. Whichever way you go, the boat should arrive at the event in the same shape it left home.

    Roof rack

    Hull upside down on padded bars, bow forward. Spars inside the car or in a bag on the rack.

    Road trailer

    Easier for long trips and two boats. Check the lights, tyres and the hull supports sit on bulkheads.

    Strapping

    Two straps over the hull, never over the gunwale edge without padding. Twist the strap once to stop it humming at motorway speed.

    Kit list

    Start with what keeps you warm and safe. Speed comes from time on the water, not from the newest gear.

    • Buoyancy aid (cut for hiking)
    • Wetsuit or drysuit, plus hiking pants
    • Dinghy boots with good grip
    • Gloves and a spare pair
    • Watch with a countdown timer
    • Water bottle and food for long days

    A guide for parents

    Your first open meeting weekend is mostly logistics: where to park, where to stay, where everyone eats, and what to do while racing is on.

    The full parents' guide (where to stay, eat and watch from) lives in our Weymouth venue guide.

    • Bring a folding chair, coffee and a warm coat. Days are long.
