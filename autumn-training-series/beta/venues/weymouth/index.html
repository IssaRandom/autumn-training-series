Weymouth & Portland

The venue guide we wanted when we started sailing here: what each wind direction really does in the harbour and the bay, how to set up the boat for it, and everything parents need for the weekend.

  • Venue WPNSA, Osprey Quay
  • Areas Portland Harbour · Weymouth Bay

The short version

  • Same wind direction, different answer: the harbour and the bay sail like two venues.
  • In a north-easterly in the bay, the top left has paid more often than not.
  • In a south-westerly the swell decides your technique, tack by tack.
  • Arrive the night before: registration and the slipway get busy early.

At a glance

WPNSA sits on Osprey Quay inside Portland Harbour. Most ILCA events race either inside the harbour walls (flat water, bendy breeze) or out in Weymouth Bay (more swell, steadier pressure). Knowing which one you’re in changes almost everything.

Which side pays, wind by wind

Pick the forecast direction. Each card says where you’re likely to race, which side has paid, and what to change in the boat. These are our notes from years of training and racing here, not guarantees: think percentages, not rules.

Offshore breeze off the land: puffy, with big pressure differences across the course. The top left has come out more often than not, but it’s a percentage play, not a certainty. Keep your head out of the boat and connect the pressure.

Watch for lanes of pressure coming off the shore.

A harbour-only feature. On some days the breeze comes in sticky blocks of pressure with almost nothing between them. When that happens, get into a block and stay in it: it won’t fill in behind you.

On the left there’s a wind bend. Sail into it and it lifts you, but overstand and you’ll pop out the other side too high. See pin 3 on the map.

Tide and swell

Inside the harbour the tide is weak. Out in the bay it matters more on the right-hand side of the course and near the Portland end. Swell wraps round the Bill in a south-westerly and arrives at an angle to the wind.

Launching and ashore

Slipway

Wide slipway straight into the harbour. Busy before the first start: rig early.

Boat park

Allocated by class at most events. Bring a trolley tag with your sail number.

Parking

Pay car park on Osprey Quay. Trailers to the overflow area.

Changing

Changing rooms and showers in the main building. Hot water runs out after racing.

Parents’ guide: the weekend

What an event day looks like from the shore, so you know when you’re needed, when you can grab a coffee, and roughly what the whole trip will cost (calculator further down).

  1. 07:30Arrive and parkThe car park fills fast on the first morning.
  2. 08:00RegistrationUsually the morning of day 1 or the evening before.
  3. 09:30BriefingSailors only. Prime time to sort lunch bags and find the coffee.
  4. 10:55First warning signalBoats leave the slipway about an hour before.
  5. 16:00Back ashoreHelp wash down, then results go up online.

Beds, food and the best seat in the house

Stay: Portland

Closest to the club. Self-catering on Osprey Quay and around Castletown.

Stay: Weymouth

More choice and restaurants, 10–15 minutes’ drive.

Eat

Café at the club for breakfast; harbourside in Weymouth for dinner. Book on Saturday nights.

Watch

Racing in the harbour is visible from the sea wall. For the bay, the view from the Nothe is best.

  • Folding chair, flask and a proper coat: the quay is “bracing”.
  • Cash or card for the car park.
  • Spare kit and a towel for the drive home.
