## Sailing: by wind direction

### North to north-east: HARBOUR
**Inner loop / first beat (close to the middle wall)**
- Watch for **persistent-shift-style blocks with pressure**. Not every day, but often enough, especially racing down by the middle wall.
- Longer-phase shifts: roughly **20° either way**, and they can last. The pressure brings the shift and it stays in.
- Water is **flatter on the right**, so there's a temptation to go hard right. It rarely works: the breeze **drops off the land on the left**, so the left is more consistent for pressure.
- **Left is the percentage play: about 60:40, maybe 65:35.** Only on the first beat.
- Phase is unpredictable: could be a 30-second, 35° swing, or one 15-minute shift that's the only one on the beat. Holes in the pressure are bigger and less stable, so it's easy to get badly caught.
- Roughly 1–1.5 meaningful shifts per beat.

**Second beat / outer loop**
- More or less a coin toss.
- You're almost directly downwind of the Nothe, with fairly high land on the left and well-mixed air, so it's normal shifty, pressure-driven sailing.
- More opportunities: 2–4 things per beat. **More predictable than the inner loop** (because it's just random, dropping breeze).

**When it's to the right of the Nothe / clocks east of north**
- Once it's got any east in it, it's a bit more persistent again. Left of the Nothe: random.

### North-west through north-east: BAY
- Same direction as the harbour, but **sea state changes** and you **don't get the persistent blocks**.
- Hard to predict, like the inner loop: the land/cliffs are high, so pressure **bounces down**. What you see is not what you get. Sailing to pressure you can see upwind is probably wrong.
- Priority: **be in the pressure first, then attack the lift**, not sail through pressure to reach a shift. Be ready to get dunked.
- Sail your own beat: what's working for you on one side isn't happening everywhere. Someone will pop out of the other side.
- Courses are usually much bigger in the bay.
- An old venue guide said **top left pays about 70:30** in a bay northerly. James & Kai: top left does seem to pay a bit more than the right; mid-beat down, no difference either way.
- Inner vs outer loop: no difference. Anything from NW to NE is the same: dropping down off the land. *"Head out the boat, look confident."*
- Cold, blustery northerlies: haphazard, shifts all over the place.

### East through south: BAY (the favourite)
- **Speed day.** Swell is the story.
- Think about **swell angle**, especially downwind. **Downwind speed is the number-one priority**; understand the wave angle and how it changes your downwind decisions.
- Longer-phase oscillations.
- **Tide:** neaps, ignore it. Springs and racing close to the harbour entrances, think about it.

### South-west to west: BAY (offset / cross swell)
- Classic offset swell is here, not in E–S: the swell **wraps round Portland Bill and then again round the harbour wall**, so it keeps bending further round.
- Where they race the UKLA qualifiers, **WSW can give full cross swell**. The further south the breeze goes in this range, the less cross swell.
- Usually **bow-on to the swell on starboard**.
- In the classic WSW, the trap is **getting greedy on port**: read the phase of the shift to judge whether you can drop the bow, go low on port and get over boats. Usually better to *save up, save up*, then drop the bow. High risk.
- Generally: not easy in the bay.

### South-west to west: HARBOUR
- **Left.** Top right never comes in. (Bottom left doesn't always come out, but top right never comes in.)
- **Inner loop:** play the left but don't hammer it. The right always looks fine... then 200 m on there's an extra 10° of left that never goes away, and slightly less breeze. Random enough that you'd try the right several times before giving up.
- **Outer loop:** just left. **The gate you round with a right turn** (right-hand mark looking downwind; sends you left) almost every time, unless you're creating leverage on pressure, and then you have to get back left.

### South: HARBOUR (occasional; "they should never race you here")
- **Big dead spot behind the Bill.** Breeze wraps round both sides and flicks from one side to the other. Too messy to diagram.
- It's never truly southerly. Pick your poison (which side you think will prevail) and **don't go middle**.

### South-east: HARBOUR
- **Left, along the wall, is strong: about 85:15.** Always a bit more breeze along the wall (same when wing-foiling/WASZP sailing and struggling to foil).
- Not always a huge gain. You can get up to about **10 boat lengths** from the wall; that's the maximum gain.

### Forecast and live wind (James, 16 Nov)
- **Live readings on the wall over-read by about 4 kts** typically, so conditions can look windier than they are.
- Forecast: **ECMWF** and **GFS**. The newer Met Office **UKV2** `[?]` (UKV?) works quite well, but James is still experimenting with it.
- Models available on most platforms: Windguru, windy.app, Windy.com.
- On page: `#forecast` intro + "The wall over-reads" callout. `[?]` which Navigator station is "the wall".

### Seasonal
- **Fog in spring (April) and autumn (late November).** Rolls in; plan for postponements.

## Parents' guide

### Stay
- **RYA Portland House** (rya.org.uk/membership-benefits/rya-portland-house): number one if you can get it. RYA members only; books up fast over weekends and big events.
- **Motorhome / campervan on site** at WPNSA, electric hook-up available. Book via WPNSA website (wpnsa.org.uk/event/campervans/) or reception. (WPNSA page lists vans only; old 2010 guide said no tents.)
- Weymouth side: far more choice. Caravan park, independent Airbnbs / Booking.com (nice ones are pricey).
- **Not recommended by us:** the Aqua, the Heights, Portland Lodge. Decided 16 Nov: not named on site; a light joke instead.

### Eat and drink
- **Weymouth town** is where to eat.
- **Nautico Lounge** (Weymouth): good craic; best breakfast/lunch and best coffee in Weymouth (Shanice's pick).
- Dinner: the **pub restaurants on the seafront / by the town bridge** (harbourside end). Good zone.
- Indian: **Shalim’s Balti House**, just a really good Indian.
- Coffee: **Beehive**, **Riptide**, **Finca** ("the most London place in Weymouth"), **Caffeine 44**.

### Watch
- **Billy Winters** (Portland): coffee and you can watch the racing. Cold in winter.

### Working on your laptop
- **Weymouth Library**: actually quite nice. Great George Street, DT4 8NN. Mon–Wed & Fri 10–5, Thu 10–6, Sat 10–4, Sun closed (Dorset Council, checked 16 Nov 2026).
- **Salt** at Portland Marina: quiet, close to the club.
- Riptide: too small to work in. Beehive: busy. Both loud.
- Spinnakers was the usual option.
